This is a conceptual sequence, not a recorded execution or a timer. A and B name complete states; C0 and C1 label opaque storage cursors, not event numbers or comparable counters. The example follows a small incremental batch with deferred persistence. By default, pending changes flush at 1,024 changed entities or after 250 ms; an explicit flush_trees() also catches up and persists maintained trees.

Read the sequence without playback

Before polling a canonical commit, Node invalidates maintained reads and wakes its worker. After the commit, the worker reconciles a private candidate from canonical state, validates its source boundary, and publishes the complete version. Persistence may follow later in one atomic tree transaction. If the process stops first, the next Node opens the durable tree and reconciles from its saved cursor. A missing or invalid recovery boundary requires a full reconstruction.

Reconciliation uses the latest visible canonical states, including adopted snapshots. Limited selections check changed IDs and refill only a missing suffix at the same source boundary. A failed source validation publishes no partial candidate and can be retried. This figure assumes validation and the eventual tree flush succeed.
